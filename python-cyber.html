Global Cybersecurity Threat Analysis

Exploring attack costs, threat sources, defense mechanisms, and industry exposure using incident-level data.

The business question

Cybersecurity teams need to understand which attack types carry the greatest financial impact, how attack sources change, and which defense mechanisms are associated with faster incident resolution.

My role & approach

I completed this individual Python project using two related datasets covering cybersecurity incidents in 2023 and 2024. I merged incident and detail files using an incident identifier, reviewed data types and missing values, and conducted exploratory and statistical analysis.

Findings

In this sample, SQL injection had the highest average financial loss at more than $56 million, followed by man-in-the-middle attacks at more than $53 million. AI-based detection had an average resolution time of 35.4 hours, more than six hours faster than firewall-dependent organizations. However, the ANOVA p-value for defense mechanism and resolution time was approximately 0.84, so the observed difference was not statistically significant in this sample.

Challenges & limitations

Combining separate files into a consistent dataset; working with a relatively small sample of 100 records; and distinguishing descriptive patterns from statistically significant results. The findings are not proof of causation or universal industry trends.

Business value

The analysis illustrates how incident data can help organizations frame questions about risk exposure and response operations. It also demonstrates why uncertainty, sample size, and statistical significance matter before turning patterns into recommendations.

Key takeaway: The strongest analysis is not simply a set of numbers. It explains what the evidence suggests, what it does not prove, and why the distinction matters.
