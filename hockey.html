The Truth Behind Playoff Hockey

Testing whether goals scored or goalie save percentage better explained NHL playoff qualification.

Comparison based on the project’s reported AIC values; lower is better.

The analytical question

Does goaltending explain NHL playoff qualification better than a team’s ability to score goals? I tested a hockey intuition using data rather than relying on conventional wisdom.

My role & methods

I completed this individual academic analysis in R, combining hockeyR play-by-play information with MoneyPuck goalie statistics across the 2021–22, 2022–23, and 2023–24 NHL seasons. I prepared team-season data and fit logistic regression models predicting playoff qualification.

Findings

The goals-scored model had an AIC of 54.31, compared with 83.35 for the save-percentage model. Because lower AIC indicates better relative model fit, goals scored provided the stronger explanation among the two models evaluated. This challenged my initial expectations.

Challenges & limitations

Combining sources required aligning teams and seasons. The analysis covers only three seasons, and team-level goalie aggregates can conceal differences among individual goaltenders. The models establish associations, not causation; AIC is a relative model-fit measure, not a standalone prediction accuracy score.

Business value

Sports teams make consequential decisions under uncertainty. This project demonstrates how to evaluate competing explanations, compare statistical models, and communicate a counterintuitive finding in a way that decision-makers can understand.
