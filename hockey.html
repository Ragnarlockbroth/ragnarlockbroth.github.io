The Truth Behind Playoff Hockey

Do goalies decide who makes the playoffs? Comparing goaltending and goal scoring across three NHL seasons.

3 seasons2021–22 to 2023–24
54.31AIC · goals model
83.35AIC · save percentage model

The business question

I wanted to test whether strong goaltending predicts playoff qualification and compare that relationship with team goal scoring. The question grew from following NHL and University of Denver hockey.

My approach

I combined NHL play-by-play data from the hockeyR R package with MoneyPuck goalie statistics for the 2021–22, 2022–23, and 2023–24 seasons. I cleaned and joined the data, explored goalie performance, created playoff indicators, and fit logistic regression models.

Results

Both save percentage and goals scored showed positive relationships with playoff qualification. The goals-scored model had a lower AIC (54.31) than the save-percentage model (83.35), suggesting it fit the data better relative to model complexity. This challenged my initial hypothesis.

Challenges & limitations

Joining datasets and aligning team and season identifiers required additional preparation. The analysis covered only three seasons; team-level goalie aggregates can hide differences between starters and backups; and playoff indicator construction may introduce bias.

Business value

Sports organizations use analytics to evaluate roster construction and performance. This project shows how statistical modeling can test an intuitive belief, compare explanations, and communicate limitations. The same evidence-based approach applies in business.

Key takeaway: The strongest analysis is not simply a set of numbers. It explains what the evidence suggests, what it does not prove, and why the distinction matters.
